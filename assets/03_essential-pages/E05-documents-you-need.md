# Documents You Need to Order Number Plates

Before a registered number plate supplier can supply road-use plates, the DVLA requires them to be satisfied about **who you are** and **that you can use the registration**. You need evidence of both.

## 1. Proof of your name and address

Any **one** of these confirms both your name and your address:

- driving licence
- utility, Council Tax or rates bill from the last 6 months
- bank or building society statement from the last 6 months
- national identity card

These confirm your **name only**, so if you use one of them you also need one of the documents above that shows your address:

- passport (it doesn't have to be UK-issued)
- bank or building society debit or credit card
- police warrant card
- armed forces identity card

## 2. Proof you can use the registration

Any **one** of:

- V5C registration certificate (log book)
- new keeper slip (the green slip)
- V750 certificate of entitlement
- V778 retention document
- V11 renewal reminder for vehicle tax or SORN
- V379 temporary registration certificate
- V948 number plate authorisation certificate with an official DVLA or DVSA stamp
- eV948 electronic number plate authorisation certificate
- a letter of authorisation from a fleet operator (including a lease or hire company) quoting the document reference number from the registration certificate
- for fleets in the V5C on-demand scheme, a PDF of the vehicle's details from the DVLA view vehicle record service

If your V5C has been lost, stolen or damaged you can [apply for a duplicate on GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vehicle-log-book).

### Getting plates made is not the same as assigning or displaying a registration
A V750 or V778 can be accepted as evidence of your entitlement to a registration even if it hasn't yet been put on a vehicle. That is separate from assigning the registration to a vehicle, which the DVLA handles, and from displaying it, which you should only do on the vehicle it belongs to. You don't have to be a vehicle's registered keeper to order, but you do need to show your own identity and an accepted entitlement document. If you're ordering on someone else's behalf, contact us first.

## 3. Original documents, electronic authorisations and photographs

The DVLA's guidance says a supplier will need to see original documents. Some authorisations are issued electronically by the DVLA, such as an eV948, and those are recognised as electronic documents. A photograph of a paper document is not the same thing. We'll tell you how to provide your documents when you order, and we won't supply your plates until our checks are complete.

## 4. What happens next

1. You place your order.
2. We check your documents, and may ask for more.
3. Once our checks are complete, your plates are made and supplied.
4. We dispatch them by Royal Mail, or confirm your collection.

If we can't complete the checks, we can't supply your plates. We'll tell you, cancel the order and refund what you've paid.

We record details of the documents we check and keep that record for three years, because the DVLA requires it. How we handle your documents, and how long we keep them, is set out in our [privacy policy](/privacy).

Knowingly providing false documents may be a criminal offence. Our policy is to accept orders only from people aged 18 or over.
